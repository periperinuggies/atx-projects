TRAVERSE Trial — TRT & Cardiovascular Safety
NEJM, 2023  ·  n=5,246, 33-month RCT  ·  Landmark
Largest RCT of testosterone therapy in hypogonadal men with elevated CV risk. TRT did not increase major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) vs placebo. However, non-fatal atrial fibrillation (+3.5%), pulmonary embolism (+1.0%), and acute kidney injury were modestly higher in the TRT arm. Small absolute risks, but worth flagging.
Your data: You're on TRT for secondary hypogonadism. This trial is direct reassurance on overall CV safety. The AF and PE signals are relevant given your Hb is rising — elevated red cell mass increases blood viscosity and clot risk. Worth keeping Hb under watch.

ApoB Outperforms LDL-C for CVD Risk
Lancet / ESC 2021 Guidelines, multiple meta-analyses
Each LDL particle contains exactly one ApoB molecule. High particle count with apparently normal LDL-C (called discordance) significantly underestimates cardiovascular risk — especially common after metabolic changes like starting TRT. ESC now recommends ApoB as the primary CVD risk marker. Target: <65 mg/dL for high-risk individuals, <80 for moderate risk.
Your data: LDL-C 3.4 — at ceiling and rising since 2022. TRT can shift lipid particle composition toward smaller, denser particles. You could have discordance where LDL-C looks borderline but ApoB reveals significantly higher true risk. This is the single most important missing test.

Optimal Oestradiol Range in Men on TRT
J Clin Endocrinol Metab, 2014–2023 (multiple studies)
Men need oestradiol for bone mineral density, cognitive function, libido, mood, and cardiovascular protection. Aggressively suppressing E2 with aromatase inhibitors (as was common practice) causes bone loss, depression, erectile dysfunction, and joint pain. The current consensus: target 70–150 pmol/L — not as low as possible. Your E2 is just above this optimal window.
Your data: E2 159 pmol/L — improving from 198. Just above the 150 ceiling of the optimal window. A modest, low-dose AI protocol could bring you to 90–120 pmol/L without overcorrecting. The key is titration — crashing E2 creates worse symptoms than mild elevation.

Enclomiphene — Fixing the HPG Axis Root Cause
Clinical Endocrinology, 2019–2024 (ongoing trials)
Enclomiphene citrate is a SERM that blocks E2 feedback at the hypothalamus, prompting the pituitary to release more LH and FSH, which drives natural testosterone production. Unlike TRT, it preserves testicular function, fertility, and the natural HPG axis. Evidence shows it can normalise T in secondary hypogonadism — exactly your Feb 2024 diagnosis (LH=2, low T, normal prolactin).
Your data: Your hypogonadism was confirmed secondary — the pituitary wasn't signalling adequately. Enclomiphene targets the cause rather than bypassing it. Not a recommendation to switch, but worth raising with your prescriber, particularly if you ever want to preserve fertility or come off TRT.

DunedinPACE — Measuring Your Rate of Ageing
Nature Aging, 2022  ·  24-year longitudinal cohort
DunedinPACE is an epigenetic clock that measures not your biological age, but how fast you are currently ageing — a "pace of ageing" score. The rate of change independently predicts mortality, cognitive decline, and physical function, regardless of your starting point. Crucially, the pace can change with interventions (diet, exercise, sleep, metabolic health).
Your data: No epigenetic testing done yet. Your hs-CRP normalisation (17.0 → 0.91) and improving fasting glucose (5.0 → 4.8) suggest markers of accelerated ageing are trending in the right direction. A single DunedinPACE test (~$350 AUD via TruDiagnostic from a blood draw) would give a biological age baseline worth tracking annually.

JUPITER Trial — hs-CRP as a Treatment Trigger
NEJM, 2008  ·  n=17,802  ·  Still guideline-setting
JUPITER showed that men with normal LDL-C but elevated hs-CRP (>2 mg/L) had a 44% relative reduction in major CV events with rosuvastatin 20mg. This established hs-CRP as an independent treatment trigger — not just a risk marker — even when LDL appears acceptable by standard guidelines.
Your data: In Feb 2025, you had hs-CRP 3.13 with LDL 3.4 — this exact combination would have met JUPITER criteria for statin consideration. Your CRP has now normalised to 0.91 (below the threshold). The question is whether it stays there, and what ApoB will show. If ApoB comes back high, the combined signal strengthens the case for statin therapy.
