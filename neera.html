The Levers — Ranked by Impact

If you change one thing, change this

  1. Lose 10kg. This is the highest-leverage intervention available. It directly improves FEV1, reduces asthma exacerbation frequency, lowers CV risk, reduces joint load, and improves insulin sensitivity. 10kg is 11.5% of body weight — clinically meaningful at every threshold. At your height, 10kg gets you to BMI 31.5 (still overweight but out of Class II). 20kg gets you to BMI 27.3 — near-healthy range. The evidence for each 5kg lost is measurable in lung function, inflammation markers, and quality of life.
  2. Fix Vitamin D. Supplement to 75+ nmol/L. This costs almost nothing, takes 8–12 weeks, and directly protects your bones (which are silently losing density right now) and helps your airways. Target: 1000–2000 IU/day with a calcium-containing meal. Retest in 3 months.
  3. Get a DEXA scan. You don't know your bone density. Given surgical menopause + VitD deficiency + age 47, you may already have osteopenia or early osteoporosis. Knowing the number gives you a baseline and may change the treatment conversation with Dr Petterson.
  4. Request HbA1c + fasting insulin at next blood panel. Catch insulin resistance before it becomes diabetes — especially important with surgical menopause-driven metabolic changes.
  5. Structured low-impact exercise. Swimming or cycling — both protect joints, build cardiorespiratory fitness (VO2max), and don't trigger exacerbations the way uphill walking does. Even 3× 20 minutes/week produces measurable mortality benefit within 3 months.

Fitness & Health Goals

Tailored to your specific picture: surgical menopause, asthma, eczema, and starting from a low fitness base. The goal is building safely — not a crash programme.

Where to Start

  • Start with 20–30 minute walks daily. No gym required, easy on lungs. Build the habit first.
  • Swimming is ideal for your situation — low impact, full body, warm air helps asthma, no grip required.
  • Aim for 150 min/week moderate movement as your first milestone (WHO minimum for health benefit).
  • Always have Ventolin within reach during exercise. Warm up slowly — cold starts trigger asthma.

Bone Density (Priority)

  • Surgical menopause + Vitamin D deficiency = elevated osteoporosis risk. This is your most important long-term health goal to address.
  • Weight-bearing exercise (walking, light resistance) is clinically proven to maintain bone density.
  • Ask Dr Petterson about a Vitamin D supplement dose and a baseline DEXA scan (bone density scan). Should be on record given your history.
  • Calcium intake matters too — aim for dairy, leafy greens, or a calcium supplement if diet is low.

Weight & Metabolic Health

  • Surgical menopause shifts fat distribution to the abdomen — this is hormonal, not just lifestyle. Your HRT helps counteract this.
  • Focus on anti-inflammatory eating — reduces eosinophilia drivers and helps eczema. Less processed food, more whole foods, omega-3 rich fish, colourful vegetables.
  • Avoid crash dieting — preserving muscle mass is more important than rapid weight loss, especially post-menopause.
  • A modest 5–10% weight reduction (4–9kg) would meaningfully reduce joint load, improve asthma control, and lower cardiovascular risk.

Asthma & Exercise

  • Dupixent is addressing the eosinophilic component — if asthma control improves over next 6 months, exercise tolerance should improve too.
  • Indoor swimming, cycling, or low-intensity walking are the most asthma-friendly options.

Vitamin D Action Plan

  • 37 nmol/L is mild deficiency — easy to fix. Ask Dr Petterson for a loading dose recommendation.
  • 10–15 min of direct sun on arms/legs daily (before 10am or after 3pm in Perth summer) will help.
  • Standard supplement: 1000–2000 IU Vitamin D3 daily is commonly recommended — confirm dose with GP.
  • Retest in 3 months to see if levels have improved to >75 nmol/L (optimal, not just sufficient).
