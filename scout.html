Investment Thesis 8 criteria
▼
What we're looking for
Model
Companies that insert themselves into an existing flow — payment clip or information flow. The pipe, not the end product. Once embedded, painful to remove.
Stage
Seed and Series A with traction. Paying customers and revenue moving — not just an idea. Fewer customers, higher revenue per customer preferred.
Market
Underserved or traditionally-served markets ripe for disruption. Global portability required — same solution must work across multiple markets.
Sectors
Fintech in any vertical. Any tech where money or information flows through the platform. No hardware, gambling, alcohol, or adult content.
Moat
Switching costs (embedded in flow, painful to remove) + first mover in an underserved market incumbents ignored. Better tech alone is not a moat.
Founder
Serial winner or deep domain operator. Lived the problem, knows the market, has done it before. Second-time founder with successful exit is the ideal profile.
Geography
Southeast Asia, South Korea, Japan, Europe, UK, AU/NZ. No USA, India, Bangladesh, South America, China, or Middle East.
Structure
B2B preferred. B2B with channel partnerships is ideal. B2C considered if the flow model is strong. Equity-focused, cheque size flexible.

Success Patterns

What the breakout alumni had in common — and the conditions that made them work
01
They owned the pipe, not the product
The most durable alumni weren't building the thing people used — they built the layer everything else ran through. Wonde doesn't teach kids. Clever doesn't run apps. SeedFlex doesn't run the merchant's business. They own the flow. The end-user product gets replaced; the pipe rarely does.
Wonde Clever SeedFlex Stripe
02
Underserved market + timing window
The breakouts didn't beat incumbents in crowded markets — they moved into spaces incumbents were ignoring or had written off as too small or too hard. Bare into funerals. SeedFlex into SEA merchant lending. The window matters: enter when the market is just large enough to prove the model but before capital floods in.
Bare Cremations SeedFlex EdStart JustFund
03
Founders who lived the problem
Almost every breakout had a founder who spent years inside the industry before starting the company. They weren't disrupting from the outside — they had been frustrated by the problem for a long time. This gives them distribution (they know the buyers), credibility (they speak the language), and conviction (they've validated the pain personally).
Domain expertise Operator background Prior exit
04
Macro tailwind they didn't create
The best outcomes rode structural forces that were already in motion — rising defence budgets, AI adoption normalising, digital payments penetration in emerging markets, regulatory pressure on legacy industries, post-COVID remote infrastructure build-out. The company didn't cause the wave. They were positioned for it.
AI normalisation Defence budgets ↑ Fintech regulation Digital payments SEA
05
Unit economics worked before scaling
The breakout Series A companies had positive unit economics at the customer level before they hit the accelerator. They weren't buying growth. Revenue per customer was high relative to CAC, churn was low because switching was painful, and the path to profitability was a function of volume, not a pivot. SeedFlex turned profitable in Malaysia before expanding.
Low churn High LTV/CAC Profitable at unit level
06
Global model, local entry
Companies that scaled internationally started with a tight geographic wedge — one country, one vertical, one customer type — but were built from day one with a model that could replicate. The playbook wasn't different in each market; the go-to-market was. Prove it in AU or SG, then export the model. Single-market architecture is a ceiling.
Wonde: UK → AU SeedFlex: MY → ID GO1: AU → Global
